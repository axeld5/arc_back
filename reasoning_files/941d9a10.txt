Here’s how I would solve it, based on the input/output pairs:

- The grid is 10x10, with 0 representing empty cells and 5 representing walls. The outputs replace certain connected empty regions with labels 1, 2, and 3.
- The transformation is clearly a flood-fill over 4-connected neighbors (up, down, left, right). You can tell it’s 4-connected because the fill never crosses diagonals and respects straight walls of 5s.

From the examples:
- Label 1 always occupies the top-left open region. In all outputs, the contiguous area reachable from (0, 0) without crossing 5s is filled with 1s.
- Label 2 always fills the central region enclosed by the 5 “grid” lines. In the examples, the 5s form horizontal lines and vertical lines that carve the board into blocks; the middle block (around the center of the grid) becomes 2s. Seeding a single center cell might sometimes sit on a wall, so use a 2x2 cluster of seeds centered in the grid to guarantee you land inside that central chamber regardless of how the walls align.
- Label 3 always fills the bottom-right open region. In all outputs, the contiguous empty area reachable from (size-1, size-1) gets filled with 3s.

So the plan:
1. Implement a standard recursive (or stack-based) flood fill that:
   - Returns immediately if out of bounds.
   - Returns if the current cell is non-zero (i.e., either wall 5 or already labeled).
   - Otherwise, writes the label into the cell and recurses to its four neighbors.
2. Fix the grid size at 10 (as per the given data).
3. Perform three fill passes in this order:
   - Seed (0, 0) with label 1 to fill the top-left reachable region.
   - Seed the four center-adjacent cells with label 2 to ensure we capture the middle chamber even if one of the exact center cells is a wall. For size 10, these four seeds are the 2x2 block at rows 4 and 5, columns 4 and 5.
     - A compact way to generate those four coordinates is to iterate index = 0..3 and compute:
       - row = size//2 - 1 + (index % 2)
       - col = size//2 - 1 + (index // 2)
   - Seed (size-1, size-1) with label 3 to fill the bottom-right reachable region.

This exactly matches the observed outputs:
- The 1 flood from the top-left stops at 5 walls and matches the 1s in the top-left zones.
- The 2 flood from the 2x2 center seeds expands into the central compartment enclosed by the 5 lines.
- The 3 flood from the bottom-right fills the bottom-right compartment only.

The function returns the mutated grid after these fills.
