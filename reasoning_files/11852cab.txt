Here’s how I would solve it from just the examples.

1) What pattern is happening in the I/O
- The outputs always “complete” a 4-fold symmetric ring around some interior pivot cell.
- The four symmetric positions come in three specific rings around a center:
  - Diagonals at distance 1: (row±1, col±1)
  - Orthogonals at distance 2: (row±2, col), (row, col±2)
  - Diagonals at distance 2: (row±2, col±2)
- In the examples, two of those rings are already complete with four identical non-zero values, and the third ring has exactly one cell with a non-zero value. The output fills the other three cells of that “one-clue” ring with the same value, without touching any non-zeros.

2) Constraints we can infer
- We only care about interior centers where all three rings are fully inside the grid. Since we look up to 2 steps away, valid centers must be at least 2 cells away from the border.
- We only consider centers that are themselves non-zero. That’s where the symmetry “anchors.”
- For any ring to be acceptable:
  - Either all four positions are filled and they all have the same value, or
  - Exactly one of the four positions is filled (non-zero) and the other three are zero.
  - Any other mix (e.g., two or three filled, or multiple different values) is invalid and we skip that center.

3) Decision and fill rule
- For a given center, check each of the three rings:
  - Collect the four values from the ring.
  - If the set of non-zero values is empty or has more than one distinct value, reject this center.
  - Record whether the ring is “full” (4 identical non-zeros) or “single” (exactly one non-zero).
- We only act when exactly one of the three rings is a “single,” and the other two are “full.” That means the puzzle gives us enough context to safely propagate the single value.
- Choose that “single” ring, take its lone non-zero value as the color, and fill the other three cells in that ring with that value. Only write into zeros; never overwrite existing non-zeros.

4) One-step solver behavior
- As soon as we find the first center that satisfies the above and perform the fill, return the updated grid. If no center qualifies, return the grid unchanged.

5) Implementation sketch (how I’d structure it)
- Predefine the three rings as lists of relative offsets from the center:
  - Ring A: diagonals at distance 1
  - Ring B: orthogonals at distance 2
  - Ring C: diagonals at distance 2
- Iterate centers over rows and cols from 2 to n-3 (inclusive) to keep rings in-bounds.
- Skip if center is zero.
- For each ring:
  - Read its four cells and validate the “single or full and consistent” rule.
  - Track count (1 or 4) and the color.
- If exactly one ring has count 1:
  - Fill that ring’s four positions with the color, but only where the grid is zero.
  - Return immediately.
- If no eligible center is found, return the input grid.

This exactly matches the examples:
- In each case, there is a center where two rings are already complete and one ring has a single clue; the solver fills the three missing cells of that ring with the same value, preserving all non-zeros, and stops after the first such fill.
