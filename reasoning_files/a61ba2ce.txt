1) Infer what the inputs contain
- The 13×13 input grid is mostly zeros with a few small “L” shapes made of three identical, non‑zero numbers. Each “L” is three cells of a 2×2 block (i.e., three corners of a 2×2, one missing), so I’ll think of them as “3-of-4” 2×2 squares.
- In each example, there are exactly four such L‑shapes, each with a different number and a different orientation:
  - One L at the top-left of its local 2×2 (cells: top-left, top-right, bottom-left filled; bottom-right missing).
  - One L at the top-right (cells: top-left, top-right, bottom-right filled; bottom-left missing).
  - One L at the bottom-left (cells: top-left, bottom-left, bottom-right filled; top-right missing).
  - One L at the bottom-right (cells: top-right, bottom-left, bottom-right filled; top-left missing).

2) Infer what the outputs represent
- The output is always a 4×4 grid.
- The four L‑shapes from the big grid are “compressed” and placed into the 4×4 in fixed positions corresponding to their orientations:
  - The top-left oriented L in the big grid becomes an L in the top-left corner of the 4×4, occupying positions (0,0), (0,1), (1,0).
  - The top-right oriented L in the big grid becomes an L in the top-right corner of the 4×4, occupying positions (0,2), (0,3), (1,3).

  - The bottom-right oriented L in the big grid becomes an L in the bottom-right corner of the 4×4, occupying positions (2,3), (3,2), (3,3).
- Everything else in the 4×4 is zero.
- This mapping exactly matches the given outputs in both examples.

3) Detection strategy
- Scan the big grid for these four specific 3-of-4 patterns, ignoring zeros. To avoid bounds issues, iterate rows and cols only up to the penultimate index (since we look at r+1 and c+1).
- For each (r, c), test the four L orientations by checking equality of three cells in the corresponding 2×2, and require they are non-zero:
  - Top-left L: (r,c), (r+1,c), (r,c+1)

  - Bottom-left L: (r,c), (r+1,c), (r+1,c+1)
  - Bottom-right L: (r+1,c+1), (r+1,c), (r,c+1)
- If any of these three-cell checks pass, we’ve found the value and orientation of one L.

- When an L is found:

  - If it’s bottom-right oriented, set result[2,3], result[3,2], result[3,3].
- Multiple detections of the same L as we scan don’t hurt; they just write the same value again in the same output slots.
