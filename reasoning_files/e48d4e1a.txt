Here’s how I would solve it, based on the input/output pairs:

- The input grids are 10x10 and contain mostly zeros, one “featured” color v that forms a plus shape (a full row of v and a full column of v that cross), and some 5s sprinkled in the grid.
- The output grids are always just that plus shape of v, but moved downward and leftward. The amount of shift equals the number of 5s in the input: shift down by count(5), and shift left by count(5). The 5s themselves are not drawn in the output.

Given that, the steps to reconstruct the outputs are:

1) Count how many 5s are in the input. That count is the translation offset used later.

2) Identify the featured color v (the one forming the plus). Since the grid is mostly zeros and only a small amount of v, v will be the least frequent non-noise value. To avoid the 5s corrupting the frequency count, treat 5s as zeros (i.e., ignore 5s when deciding the least frequent value). Then compute the least frequent value on that modified grid; that will be v.

3) Find the intersection (center) of the plus. Among the cells equal to v, pick one that:
   - Is not on the border of the grid (so it has four neighbors).
   - Has v in all four orthogonal neighbors (up, down, left, right).
   This uniquely identifies the crossing point of the full row and full column of v.

4) Compute the new center by applying the translation implied by the 5s:
   - New row = original row + count(5)  (shift down)
   - New col = original col − count(5)  (shift left)

5) Create a fresh 10x10 grid of zeros for the output. Through the translated center, draw the full horizontal and vertical lines of color v:
   - Set the entire new row to v.
   - Set the entire new column to v.

6) Return that grid. Only one such plus is drawn (stop after the first valid intersection is found).

Why this matches the examples:
- In each case, the featured color (2, 4, 6, or 3) is much less common than 0s, so it’s the least frequent non-5 value.
- The intersection point is the only interior point with same-valued neighbors in all four directions.
- The number of 5s exactly matches the translation: e.g., 2 fives => move down 2 and left 2; 3 fives => move down 3 and left 3, etc.
- The output contains only the translated plus shape, nothing else.
