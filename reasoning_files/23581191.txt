Here’s how I’d solve it from the I/O patterns:

- Observe the input always has exactly two nonzero cells, each with its own value (e.g., 8 and 7). Everything else is 0.
- In the output, each nonzero cell “paints” its entire row and its entire column with its own value.
  - So if the nonzero cells are at (r1, c1) = v1 and (r2, c2) = v2, then:
    - Every cell in row r1 and column c1 becomes v1.
    - Every cell in row r2 and column c2 becomes v2.
- The two “cross” intersections between these two lines (r1, c2) and (r2, c1) are special: they are set to 2, overriding whatever would be painted there by the row/column fills.
- All other cells (those not in these painted rows/columns) remain 0.

Algorithm I’d implement:

1) Initialize a fresh 9x9 grid of zeros for the result (we don’t need to modify the input).
2) Scan the input to collect all nonzero entries as tuples (row, col, value). From the examples, there will be exactly two.
3) For each collected (row, col, value), fill the entire corresponding row and column in the result grid with that value.
4) After all painting is done, set the two cross positions to 2:
   - Use the row from the first nonzero and the column from the second.
   - Use the row from the second nonzero and the column from the first.
5) Return the resulting grid.

This exactly matches the examples:
- The columns of the two nonzeros get filled down the whole grid.
- The rows of the two nonzeros get filled across the whole grid.
- The two cross-points between those lines are 2.
