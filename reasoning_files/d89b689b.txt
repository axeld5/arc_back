Here’s how I would solve it from the given input/output pairs:

- First, notice the invariant across all examples:
  - Each grid has exactly one 2x2 block of 8s.
  - There are exactly four other non-zero numbers elsewhere on the grid, none of which are 8.
  - The output zeros everything except the 2x2 region where the 8s were; that region gets replaced by the four non-8 numbers.
  - The placement inside the 2x2 is consistent: the top row of the 2x2 comes from the first two non-8, non-zero cells encountered in a row-major scan; the bottom row comes from the next two. Within each row, the two numbers are placed left-to-right according to their original column order.

- Strategy:
  1. Scan the grid row by row (row-major order).
  2. Collect coordinates of all cells whose values are not 0 and not 8. There should be exactly four; keep them in the order encountered.
  3. Also find the position of the first 8 encountered in the scan; treat that as the top-left corner of the 2x2 target block.
  4. Split the four collected coordinates into two pairs:
     - The first two become the sources for the top row of the 2x2.
     - The next two become the sources for the bottom row.
  5. For each pair, order the two values left-to-right by their original column indices:
     - If the first source column is greater than the second source column, swap the pair; otherwise keep as is.
  6. Write the two ordered values of the first pair into the target block’s top row (two adjacent columns starting at the anchor column).
  7. Write the two ordered values of the second pair into the target block’s bottom row.
  8. Set the original four source positions to 0.

- Implementation notes I’d follow:
  - Use a helper to collect coordinate pairs under a predicate (e.g., “value not in {0, 8}” or “value equals 8”).
  - Use enumerate to get both row/column indices and values during the scan.
  - Use slice assignment to fill the two adjacent cells in each row of the 2x2.
  - To enforce left-to-right placement within each row, compare the two source column indices and reverse the pair if the first is to the right of the second.
  - Finally, zero out the four original positions.

- Why this matches the examples:
  - In each case, the top-left 8 of the 2x2 is taken as the anchor.
  - The first two eligible numbers encountered form the top row; they’re ordered by their original columns.
  - The next two form the bottom row; again ordered by original columns.
  - All other cells are zero in the output.
