Here’s how I would solve it from the given I/O pairs:

- Observation and hypothesis
  - The yellow (value 4) cells always appear as four corner markers that define a rectangular frame. The desired output is exactly that framed region cropped out, with the pattern fully reconstructed inside it, and 4’s at the four corners.
  - Outside the yellow frame, there are small clusters of nonzero, non-4 colors. These form the “template” (the original unscaled motif).
  - Inside the yellow frame, there are some colored pixels that look like a magnified version of the template, but only partially present. The task is to infer the scaling factor and alignment from these hints and then fill in the entire magnified motif inside the frame.

- Step 1: Find the frame
  - Collect all coordinates where the color is 4.
  - Take the min/max row and column among these to get the bounding rectangle of the yellow frame.
  - Record its height and width; this is the size of the output.

- Step 2: Extract the unscaled template
  - Look at all colored cells outside that yellow rectangle, ignoring 0 and 4.
  - These define the “small” motif: compute its tight bounding box (min/max row and col among these).
  - Normalize these coordinates by translating so the top-left of this small bounding box is (0, 0). Keep a mapping from (row, col) to color for the small motif, and record its height and width.

- Step 3: Gather the interior clues
  - Inside the yellow rectangle, collect all nonzero, non-4 cells. Record them with positions relative to the top-left corner of the yellow rectangle. These are the “inside clues” that a correctly magnified and positioned template must match.

- Step 4: Infer scale and placement by consistency checking
  - Reasonable ARC-like magnification factors are small integers; try m in {2, 3, 4}.
  - For a candidate m, the magnified motif dimensions are (small_height * m, small_width * m).
  - Slide this magnified block within the frame: try all offsets (dy, dx) such that the entire magnified block stays within the yellow rectangle.
  - For each inside clue pixel at position (ir, ic) inside the frame with color col:
    - Convert it to coordinates relative to the candidate magnified block by subtracting the offset: (rr, cc) = (ir - dy, ic - dx).
    - If this lands outside the magnified block, this placement fails.
    - Otherwise, map it back to the small motif by integer downscaling: (rs, cs) = (rr // m, cc // m).
    - Check that the small motif has a colored pixel at (rs, cs) and that its color equals col. If any clue fails, reject this (m, dy, dx).
  - The first (m, dy, dx) that matches all interior clues is the solution.

- Step 5: Construct the output
  - Create an output grid whose size equals the frame’s height and width, initialized to zeros.
  - Put 4’s at its four corners.
  - For each colored cell (rs, cs) in the small motif, paint an m by m block in the output starting at (dy + rs*m, dx + cs*m) with the corresponding color.
  - Return this completed framed image.

- Why this matches the examples
  - The “small” motif is always visible outside the frame; the inside gives enough hints to determine both the scaling factor and how the magnified motif is positioned within the frame.
  - The outputs are exactly the framed region with the magnified motif filled in and 4’s at the corners, matching the target examples.
